The mark

A dial that is also a graph

The twelve tick marks double as the nodes of a small graph — a dodecagon with an inscribed hexagon for edges. The analog face the model reads and the network that reads it are the same shape. The hands sit at 10:10: the hour hand in brand blue, the minute hand in brand red, meeting at a solid hub.

The logos  /  four files

Icon and lockup, light and dark

Use the light files on grounds lighter than mid-grey, the dark files on anything darker. SVG is the source of truth; PNG exports sit alongside each one.

Palette

Three brand colors, two inks

#3a7eabBlue — hour hand, the o
#cf4832Red — minute hand, accents
#d1d3d4Grey — dial edges & nodes
#23292bInk — wordmark on light
#eef0f0Ink — wordmark on dark

On dark grounds the wordmark o lightens to #4f97c4 so it holds its own against the ink.

Using it

A few rules

+Keep clear space of at least half the icon width on every side.
+Prefer the SVG. Reach for the PNGs only where SVG will not render.
+Below ~24 px, drop the wordmark and use the icon alone.
−Don't recolor the hands or swap the blue and red.
−Don't rotate the mark or reset the hands to another time.
−Don't set the wordmark in a different typeface — it's drawn, not typed.
